Boston Event Planning Checklist

Use this checklist to turn your ideas into a practical plan for an event in Boston, Massachusetts. Start with your priorities, then organize the decisions, costs, and people needed to carry it out.

In this checklist
  • Planning timeline
  • Budget categories
  • Venue questions
  • Remote planning support

1. Build your planning timeline

These are starting points, not booking deadlines. Adjust for your event size, date, venue availability, and vendor requirements. If your event is sooner, begin with the essentials and confirm what is feasible before committing.

  1. 3–6 months ahead, or as early as possible

    • Write a short brief: occasion or business purpose, preferred date and alternatives, guest-count range, Boston location preferences, and your top three priorities.
    • Set an overall spending limit. For a celebration, agree who makes decisions; for a business gathering, confirm the budget approver and event lead.
    • Shortlist venues and key vendors. Check availability, what each quote includes, and booking terms before signing or paying.

  2. 8–12 weeks ahead

    • Confirm your venue and core vendors directly. Record deposits, balances, cancellation terms, and each supplier’s deadlines in one tracker.
    • Prepare invitations or registration details, an RSVP deadline, and a way to collect dietary and accessibility needs.
    • Identify who will manage the event onsite. Agree their responsibilities for setup, vendor arrivals, guest support, and closing the space.

  3. 4–6 weeks ahead

    • Draft the event timeline: setup, arrivals, meals, activities or presentations, and breakdown. Include the person responsible for each step.
    • Review the layout, menu, equipment, and guest travel information. Check Boston parking, transit options, loading access, and any date-specific disruptions with the venue.
    • Compare updated quotes with the budget. Resolve open decisions and agree a backup plan for any outdoor elements.

  4. 1–2 weeks ahead, subject to supplier deadlines

    • Reconcile RSVPs and confirm the final guest count, seating, dietary needs, and accessibility arrangements by the venue’s required dates.
    • Confirm vendor arrival windows, setup instructions, equipment checks, and payment deadlines. For business events, review speaker timing and presentation files.
    • Share the final run of show, contact list, layout, and outstanding tasks with your onsite lead and relevant vendors.

  5. Event week and event day

    • Reconfirm the plan with your local team, including who handles changes, vendor questions, and guest issues.
    • Keep the final documents and contact numbers accessible to the people delivering the event.
    • Have your onsite lead oversee setup, the schedule, and breakdown. Aureliya’s support is remote planning, not onsite or event-day management.

2. Give every cost a place in the budget

For each category, track the estimate, written quote, amount committed, amount paid, remaining balance, due date, and person responsible. Mark unknown costs rather than treating them as zero.

Venue
Room hire, minimum spend, setup time, overtime, cleaning, and any required security.
Food & beverages
Menu, drinks, staffing, dietary requirements, and the final head-count deadline.
Rentals & decor
Furniture, linens, tableware, flowers, signage, delivery, setup, and collection.
Technology & entertainment
Sound, microphones, screens, lighting, Wi-Fi, performers, and technical support.
Guest arrangements
Invitations or registration, transport, parking support, and any planned guest materials.
Planning & local coordination
Agreed remote planning support and separately contracted onsite staffing or coordination.
Additional charges
Ask which taxes, service charges, gratuities, insurance, or other requirements apply and whether they are included.
Contingency
Keep an uncommitted reserve for changes. Set its size around the uncertainties in your plan.

Compare the full cost, not just the headline quote. You select and contract with venues, vendors, and any local coordinator, and pay them directly.

3. Ask these questions before choosing a venue

  • Availability and access: Is the date available? What time can vendors enter, when must the event finish, and when must everything be removed?
  • Capacity and layout: Does the space fit your seated or standing guest count, food service, activities, and any presentation area?
  • Guest needs: What step-free routes, restrooms, seating options, and dietary accommodations can the venue confirm?
  • Boston logistics: Where do guests arrive, park, or connect with transit? Where can suppliers unload, and are there building or street-access restrictions?
  • Inclusions and restrictions: Which furniture, catering, equipment, staff, and cleanup are included? Are outside vendors, decor, music, or outdoor activities restricted?
  • Costs and terms: What is the total written estimate? What are the deposit, balance, cancellation, rescheduling, minimum-spend, and overtime terms?
  • Responsibility and backup plans: Who is the venue contact on the day? Who handles required permissions, insurance, weather changes, and emergency procedures?
